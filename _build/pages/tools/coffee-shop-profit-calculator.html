How the model works

Revenue is average ticket × customers per day × days open. Gross profit subtracts cost of goods — coffee, milk, cups, lids, syrups, food. Net profit then subtracts fixed monthly costs: labor, rent, other overheads and loan repayments. Break-even is the number of customers per day at which net profit reaches zero.

Benchmarks many operators use as sanity checks: rent at or below roughly 8–10% of sales, labor around 25–35% including the owner's pay, and beverage cost of goods in the 20–30% range. If your model only works with more customers than your location could plausibly serve at peak, revisit the lease or the concept. The startup costs guide and menu pricing guide go deeper.

Stress-test your plan

  1. Halve your opening traffic. New cafés often take months to reach steady volume. Can you survive at 50% of your target for six months?
  2. Add 3 points to cost of goods. Milk and coffee prices move; waste is real in year one.
  3. Pay yourself. A model that only works if the owner works for free is a job, not a business.
