How to use the wheel

  1. Smell first. Most "flavor" is aroma. Sniff the dry grounds, then the brewed cup as it cools.
  2. Start broad. Decide on a family — fruity, sweet, nutty — before reaching for a specific note.
  3. Compare two coffees. Differences are far easier to name side by side than alone. Our tasting guide shows a simple at-home cupping.

Where flavors come from

Fruit and floral notes are mostly preserved from the bean's origin and variety, so they shine in lighter roasts and in Ethiopian and Kenyan coffees. Caramel, nut and chocolate notes develop during roasting through Maillard reactions and caramelization. Fermented and boozy notes come from processing, especially naturals and anaerobic fermentations. Roasty, smoky notes grow with roast time and heat — see roast levels.
