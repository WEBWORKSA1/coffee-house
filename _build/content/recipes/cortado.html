The cortado is a small, honest drink: a double espresso and a splash of warm milk in roughly equal measure. It softens espresso's acidity and bitterness while keeping its body and flavor up front, which makes it a favorite mid-morning or after-lunch coffee.

What it is and where it comes from

The cortado has roots in Spain, where cortar means to cut, and similar drinks are common in Portugal and parts of Latin America. Spanish cafés traditionally serve it in a small glass, sometimes with less textured milk than you will find in modern specialty cafés. Regional versions abound: some add condensed milk, which links it to the drink explored in our Spanish latte recipe.

Flavor and texture

A cortado is dense and rounded. The espresso's chocolate, caramel or fruit notes are fully present, but the milk mutes any sharp edges and adds a soft sweetness. There is very little foam, so the texture is smooth and liquid rather than airy. Because it is small, it cools to drinking temperature quickly and is usually finished in a few sips.

Technique notes

Steaming small quantities of milk is tricky because most jugs are designed for larger volumes. Use the smallest jug you have and steam a little more milk than you need; the wand needs depth to create a whirlpool. Add only a brief hiss of air. Keep the temperature a touch lower than for a latte, around 55–60°C, because the drink is drunk quickly and very hot milk overwhelms delicate espresso. A strong, well-extracted shot matters more here than in larger drinks; our espresso at home guide covers the basics of dialing in.

Variations

  • Cortado condensado: add a teaspoon or two of sweetened condensed milk to the espresso before the steamed milk.
  • Iced cortado: pour a double espresso over a few ice cubes and add an equal measure of cold milk.
  • Single-shot cortado: use a single espresso and about 30 ml of milk for an even smaller cup.
  • Plant milk: oat milk keeps the drink creamy; almond can taste thin in such a small serving.

Common mistakes

Stretching the milk too much turns a cortado into a miniature cappuccino. Pouring too much milk turns it into a flat white. And using a large mug lets the drink cool too fast and look lost; size the vessel to the drink.

Serving tips

Serve in a small glass of 120–150 ml or a thick-walled espresso cup. A glass of sparkling water alongside refreshes the palate. For a café menu, the cortado is often priced between an espresso and a cappuccino, and our coffee menu pricing guide explains how cafés think about that ladder.
