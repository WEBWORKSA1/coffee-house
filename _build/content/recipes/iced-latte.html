An iced latte is the warm-weather sibling of the classic latte: espresso and cold milk over ice, with no steaming required. It is quick, creamy and refreshing, and it works just as well with a home espresso machine as with cold brew concentrate.

What it is and where it comes from

Cold coffee and milk drinks have been made for a long time in many countries, but the iced latte as a standard café menu item grew alongside espresso bar culture and the rising popularity of iced coffee drinks in recent decades. It is now one of the best-selling cold drinks on many café menus, especially in summer.

Flavor and texture

Cold milk tastes less sweet than steamed milk, so an iced latte reads as cleaner and more refreshing, with the espresso more prominent. There is no foam, just a smooth, chilled body. Bright, fruity espressos can taste like coffee and cream; chocolatey espressos taste like a rich chocolate milk with a coffee finish.

Technique notes

Build the drink in the right order: ice, sweetener, milk, then espresso. Hot espresso poured straight onto ice melts it quickly and thins the drink, while milk acts as a buffer. Use a slightly stronger, shorter shot than usual, or even a triple, because cold temperatures mute flavor. Use plenty of ice so the drink is truly cold; a half-filled glass melts faster. Variations

  • Coffee ice cubes: freeze leftover coffee in a tray so the drink gets stronger as it melts.
  • Shaken iced latte: shake espresso, syrup and ice in a jar for 10 seconds, strain, then top with milk for a frothy texture.
  • Vanilla or caramel: replace simple syrup with 15 ml of flavored syrup.
  • Cold foam top: froth 50 ml of cold skim or low-fat milk with a handheld frother and spoon it on top.
  • Oat iced latte: oat milk adds natural sweetness and a creamy body.

Common mistakes

Using a weak single shot is the most common error; the drink tastes like cold milk. Adding granulated sugar leaves gritty crystals at the bottom. Letting the drink sit too long also dilutes it, so build it right before serving.

Serving tips

Serve in a tall clear glass to show off the layers, with a reusable straw or a long spoon for stirring. To make simple syrup, dissolve equal weights of sugar and hot water and refrigerate for up to a few weeks. For a sweeter, richer iced drink, try our Vietnamese iced coffee.
