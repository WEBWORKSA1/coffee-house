Irish coffee is a warming coffee cocktail: hot, sweetened coffee spiked with Irish whiskey and crowned with a float of cool, lightly whipped cream. You sip the hot coffee through the cream, which is what makes it so memorable.

Note: This recipe contains alcohol and is intended only for adults of legal drinking age. Please drink responsibly. A non-alcoholic variation is included below.

What it is and where it comes from

Irish coffee is generally credited to the west of Ireland in the 1940s, where it is said to have been served to travelers arriving cold and tired at a transatlantic air terminal. From there it spread to bars around the world. The details of the story vary in the telling, but its pairing of Irish whiskey, sugar, strong coffee and cream has remained remarkably consistent.

Flavor and texture

The first sip combines cool, sweet cream with hot, rich coffee and the warmth and vanilla notes of the whiskey. The brown sugar adds a light caramel depth and balances the bitterness of the coffee. The texture contrast between the two layers is central, so the cream should sit on top in a distinct band rather than melt in.

Technique notes

Use well-brewed coffee rather than espresso. A medium roast made in a French press or a pour-over gives the right strength and body. Fully dissolve the sugar before adding cream; sweetened coffee is denser, which helps the cream float. Whip the cream only until it is slightly thickened, like a loose custard. Shaking it in a jar for 15–20 seconds works well. Pour it slowly over the back of a spoon held just above the surface.

Variations

  • Non-alcoholic Irish-style coffee: omit the whiskey, add 2–3 drops of vanilla extract and an extra half teaspoon of brown sugar, and float the cream as usual. Suitable for anyone.
  • Demerara or raw sugar: gives a deeper, molasses-like sweetness.
  • Spiced cream: whisk a pinch of nutmeg or cinnamon into the cream.
  • Decaf version: use decaffeinated coffee for a late-evening serving.

Common mistakes

Over-whipped cream sits like a stiff cap and does not blend pleasantly as you sip. Under-whipped cream sinks immediately. Skipping the sugar is the other classic error: the cream is far more likely to sink into unsweetened coffee. Using a cold glass also cools the drink too fast.

Serving tips

Serve in a warmed, stemmed glass so the layers are visible and the drink stays hot. Grate a little nutmeg or dark chocolate over the cream if you like. For another coffee-based dessert that needs no alcohol, try an affogato.
