Dalgona coffee is a whipped coffee foam spooned over a glass of milk. Its striking two-tone look, caramel-colored foam floating on white milk, made it a worldwide social media hit, but it is also a genuinely tasty drink that needs no special equipment.

What it is and where it comes from

The name comes from dalgona, a crunchy honeycomb toffee sold as a street snack in South Korea, which the foam resembles in color and flavor. Whipped instant coffee itself has a longer history in several countries; in parts of South Asia and Greece, for example, versions made by beating instant coffee with sugar have been enjoyed for decades. The name dalgona and the global craze took off around 2020.

Flavor and texture

The foam tastes intensely of coffee and caramelized sugar, with a slightly bitter edge that the milk softens. On the spoon it is thick, fluffy and almost like mousse. Once stirred into milk, the drink becomes a sweet, creamy, strongly flavored coffee milk.

Technique notes

The key is equal volumes of instant coffee, sugar and hot water. Instant coffee works because it contains a high concentration of dissolved coffee solids that, combined with sugar, can trap air and hold a foam. Brewed coffee lacks those solids. An electric mixer makes the job easy; by hand, use a balloon whisk and keep a steady rhythm. You are done when the foam is the color of peanut butter and holds a peak on the whisk. Variations

  • Less sweet: reduce the sugar to 1.5 tbsp and whisk a little longer.
  • Mocha dalgona: add 1 tsp of cocoa powder to the mix, or stir chocolate syrup into the milk.
  • Brown sugar dalgona: use light brown sugar for a deeper toffee note.
  • Plant milk: oat or soy milk works well underneath; the foam itself is already dairy-free.
  • Decaf: decaf instant coffee foams the same way. Our decaf explained article covers how decaf is made.

Common mistakes

Using cold water slows dissolving and makes whipping harder. Using too little coffee or too much water yields a runny foam that never peaks. Stopping too early leaves a dark, loose froth; keep going until the color lightens dramatically.

Serving tips

Serve in a clear glass so the layers show, and give it a stir once photographed. Dalgona is strong: two tablespoons of instant coffee can contain more caffeine than a typical cup, so check our caffeine calculator if you are keeping track. A dusting of cocoa or crushed honeycomb candy on top echoes the drink's namesake.
