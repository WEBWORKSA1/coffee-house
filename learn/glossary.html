Coffee Glossary: 100+ Coffee Terms Explained A to Z

Ratios, roast stages, processing methods and tasting terms, all in one place. Use this A–Z coffee glossary whenever a recipe, bag label or café menu leaves you guessing.

14 min readBy the Coffee.House editorial teamUpdated October 2026How we write

Coffee comes with its own language, borrowed from agriculture, chemistry, Italian café culture and professional tasting. This glossary defines more than 100 terms you are likely to meet on bags, menus, brewing guides and equipment listings. Definitions are written for home brewers and aspiring café owners alike, with links to our in-depth guides where a term deserves a closer look.

A

Acidity
The bright, lively, sometimes tart quality in coffee, perceived on the sides of the tongue. In tasting, acidity is usually a positive trait, described with words like citrusy, juicy, crisp or winey; it is not the same as stomach acidity or pH.
AeroPress
A plastic cylindrical brewer in which coffee steeps briefly and is then pressed through a paper or metal filter using a plunger. It is portable, quick and very flexible.
Affogato
An Italian dessert of a scoop of vanilla gelato or ice cream with a shot of hot espresso poured over it.
Aftertaste
The flavors and sensations that linger after coffee is swallowed or spit out. Also called the finish; a long, sweet aftertaste is a sign of quality.
Agitation
Any stirring, swirling or turbulent pouring that moves grounds during brewing. More agitation generally speeds up extraction.
Altitude
The elevation at which coffee is grown, usually expressed in meters above sea level. Higher, cooler farms tend to slow cherry ripening, which is often associated with denser beans and more complex acidity.
Americano
Espresso diluted with hot water, producing a drink similar in strength to filter coffee but with an espresso character.
Arabica
Coffea arabica, the species behind most specialty coffee. It is prized for sweetness, acidity and aromatic complexity and typically contains about 1.2–1.5% caffeine. Compare it in our arabica vs robusta guide.
Aroma
The smell of brewed or wet coffee. In cupping, aroma is distinguished from fragrance, which is the smell of dry grounds.

B

Barista
A person who prepares and serves espresso-based and other coffee drinks professionally. The word is Italian for bartender.
Blend
Coffee made by combining beans from two or more origins, often to create a consistent, balanced flavor year-round, especially for espresso.
Bloom
The initial pour of a small amount of water, typically about twice the weight of the coffee, that lets fresh grounds release carbon dioxide before the main pour. Fresh coffee visibly bubbles and rises during the bloom.
Body
The perceived weight and texture of coffee in the mouth, ranging from light and tea-like to heavy and syrupy. Closely related to mouthfeel.
Bottomless portafilter
An espresso portafilter with the base cut away so the underside of the basket is visible. Also called a naked portafilter, it helps diagnose channeling and uneven extraction.
Bourbon
A classic arabica variety, historically linked to the island of Réunion, known for sweetness and balance. It has many natural mutations and descendants.
Brew ratio
The proportion of coffee to water by weight, such as 1:16 for filter coffee or 1:2 for espresso. Our brew ratio calculator does the math for any batch size.
Burr grinder
A grinder that crushes beans between two abrasive surfaces, called burrs, set a precise distance apart. It produces far more consistent particles than a blade grinder.

C

Caffeine
A naturally occurring stimulant found in coffee, tea and other plants. Brewed coffee typically contains about 80–200 mg per 240 ml cup, depending on beans, dose and size.
Cappuccino
An espresso drink topped with steamed milk and a relatively thick layer of foam, traditionally served in a 150–180 ml cup.
Cascara
The dried skin and pulp of the coffee cherry, steeped like tea. It has a fruity, hibiscus-like flavor and contains some caffeine.
Channeling
A fault in espresso where water finds weak paths through the coffee puck, over-extracting some areas and under-extracting others. It often causes sour and bitter flavors in the same shot.
Cherry
The fruit of the coffee plant. Each cherry usually contains two seeds, which become coffee beans after processing.
Chemex
A glass pour-over brewer shaped like an hourglass, used with thick paper filters that produce a very clean, light-bodied cup.
Chlorogenic acids
A group of compounds abundant in green coffee that contribute to bitterness and astringency. They break down progressively during roasting.
Cold brew
Coffee made by steeping coarse grounds in cold or room-temperature water for 12–24 hours. It tastes smooth and low in bitterness; see cold brew vs iced coffee.
Cortado
A Spanish-origin drink of espresso "cut" with a roughly equal amount of warm, lightly textured milk.
Crema
The reddish-brown foam on top of a fresh espresso, formed by carbon dioxide and emulsified oils under pressure. Good crema signals fresh coffee but does not guarantee good flavor.
Cupping
A standardized method of tasting coffee by steeping grounds directly in bowls, breaking the crust and slurping from a spoon. Learn the home version in how to taste coffee.

D

Decaf
Coffee from which typically 97% or more of the caffeine has been removed, before roasting, using water, carbon dioxide or a solvent.
Degassing
The release of carbon dioxide from freshly roasted beans over the days and weeks after roasting. Excess gas can cause uneven extraction, which is why many coffees taste best after a short rest.
Dialing in
Adjusting grind, dose, yield and time to get the best flavor from a particular coffee, especially for espresso.
Direct trade
A sourcing approach in which roasters buy from specific farms or cooperatives, usually with quality premiums and long-term relationships. It has no single official definition.
Doppio
A double espresso, typically made with 16–20 g of coffee and yielding about 32–45 g of liquid.
Dose
The weight of ground coffee used for a brew.
Drip coffee
Coffee made by letting hot water flow through a bed of grounds and a filter, either by machine or by hand.

E

Espresso
A concentrated coffee made by forcing hot water, at around 9 bar of pressure, through finely ground, compacted coffee, usually in 25–35 seconds.
Extraction
The dissolving of soluble compounds from ground coffee into water. A balanced extraction typically falls around 18–22% of the coffee's weight.

F

Fair trade
A family of certification schemes that set minimum prices, community premiums and labor standards for producer organizations.
Fines
Very small particles produced during grinding. Some are unavoidable, but too many can clog filters and add bitterness.
First crack
An audible popping during roasting, typically around 195–205°C (383–401°F), when moisture and gas fracture the bean. It marks the beginning of a drinkable light roast.
Flash brew
Iced coffee brewed hot directly onto ice, chilling it immediately to preserve aroma. Also called Japanese-style iced coffee.
Flat white
A small, strong milk drink with espresso and a thin layer of velvety microfoam, associated with Australia and New Zealand.
Fragrance
The smell of dry, freshly ground coffee, assessed before water is added during cupping.
French press
An immersion brewer with a plunger and metal mesh filter that produces a full-bodied cup with some sediment.

G

Gesha
An arabica variety, also spelled Geisha, originally collected in Ethiopia and famous for intense floral and tea-like flavors. It often commands very high prices.
Gooseneck kettle
A kettle with a long, thin, curved spout that gives precise control over pour rate and placement, ideal for pour-over.
Green coffee
Unroasted coffee beans, the form in which coffee is traded and shipped internationally. Stable for months when stored properly.
Grind size
How coarse or fine coffee is ground, from extra-fine for Turkish coffee to extra-coarse for cold brew. See our grind size chart.
Group head
The part of an espresso machine where the portafilter locks in and water is dispensed onto the coffee.

H

Heirloom
A label often used for Ethiopian coffees made up of many local landrace varieties that have not been formally catalogued.
Honey process
A processing method in which the skin is removed but some or all of the sticky fruit layer is left on the bean while it dries. It typically gives sweetness and body between washed and natural styles.
Hopper
The container on top of a grinder that holds whole beans before grinding.

I

Immersion brewing
Any method in which all the grounds steep in water for the whole brew, such as French press, cupping and cold brew.
Instant coffee
Brewed coffee that has been dried, by spray-drying or freeze-drying, into soluble crystals or powder.

K

Kissaten
A traditional Japanese café known for a quiet atmosphere and carefully hand-brewed coffee.
Knock box
A sturdy container with a padded bar used to knock spent espresso pucks out of the portafilter.

L

Latte
Espresso with a generous amount of steamed milk and a thin layer of microfoam, usually served in a 240–350 ml cup.
Latte art
Patterns such as hearts, tulips and rosettas created by pouring microfoam into espresso. Start with our latte art basics.
Light roast
Coffee roasted to shortly after first crack, preserving more origin character and acidity.
Lungo
A "long" espresso pulled with more water, often a 1:3 ratio or higher, giving a lighter, more bitter-edged shot.

M

Macchiato
An espresso "marked" with a small spoonful of milk foam.
MASL
Meters above sea level, the standard unit for describing farm altitude on coffee labels.
Microfoam
Steamed milk filled with bubbles too small to see, giving a glossy, paint-like texture essential for latte art.
Microlot
A small, separately processed batch of coffee from a specific farm section, picking day or process, often sold at a premium.
Mocha
An espresso drink with chocolate and steamed milk. The name comes from the historic Yemeni port of Mocha.
Moka pot
A stovetop brewer that uses steam pressure to push water up through coffee, producing a strong, espresso-like brew.
Mouthfeel
The tactile sensation of coffee in the mouth, including body, texture, smoothness and astringency.

N

Natural process
Also called dry process, a method in which whole cherries are dried with the fruit intact before hulling. It often produces fruity, winey, heavy-bodied coffees.
Nitro cold brew
Cold brew infused with nitrogen gas and served from a tap, giving a creamy texture and cascading foam.

O

Origin
Where a coffee was grown, from country down to region, cooperative or farm.
Over-extraction
Dissolving too much from the coffee, typically from grinding too fine or brewing too long. It tastes bitter, harsh and drying.

P

Parchment
The papery layer surrounding the green bean after the fruit is removed. Coffee is often dried and rested in parchment before milling.
Peaberry
A cherry containing a single round seed instead of two flat-sided ones. Peaberries are sometimes sorted and sold separately.
Percolation
Brewing in which water flows through a bed of coffee, as in pour-over and espresso, rather than steeping in it.
Portafilter
The handled holder for the espresso basket, which locks into the group head.
Pour-over
Manual drip brewing in which hot water is poured by hand over coffee in a filter cone.
Pre-infusion
A brief, low-pressure wetting of the espresso puck before full pressure is applied, helping reduce channeling.
Puck
The compacted disc of coffee in an espresso basket, before or after brewing.

Q

Quaker
An unripe or underdeveloped bean that stays pale after roasting and adds a papery or peanut-like off-flavor.

R

Rest
The period after roasting during which beans degas and flavors settle, often a few days for filter coffee and one to two weeks for espresso.
Ristretto
A "restricted" espresso shot pulled shorter, often near a 1:1 to 1:1.5 ratio, for a sweeter, more intense taste.
Roast date
The day a coffee was roasted, the most useful freshness indicator on a bag.
Robusta
Coffea canephora, a hardy, high-yield species with roughly 2.2–2.7% caffeine and a heavier, more bitter profile than arabica.
Rosetta
A fern-like latte art pattern made by wiggling the pitcher while drawing it back across the cup.

S

Second crack
A quieter, faster crackling later in roasting, typically around 220–230°C (428–446°F), signaling the start of a dark roast.
Single origin
Coffee from one identifiable place rather than a blend of origins.
Specialty coffee
High-quality coffee that scores well in professional cupping and is defined by traceability and distinctive flavor.
Steam wand
The metal pipe on an espresso machine that injects steam into milk for heating and texturing.
Strength
How concentrated a brew is, determined mainly by the ratio of coffee to water. Measured as total dissolved solids.

T

Tamping
Pressing ground coffee evenly into the espresso basket with a tamper so water flows through uniformly.
TDS
Total dissolved solids, the percentage of a brew that is dissolved coffee, measured with a refractometer. Filter coffee often lands near 1.2–1.5%; espresso around 8–12%.
Terroir
The combined influence of soil, climate, altitude and local practices on a coffee's flavor.
Third wave
A loose label for the craft-focused movement emphasizing origin, lighter roasting and careful brewing.
Turkish coffee
Extremely fine coffee simmered in a small pot and served unfiltered with a foam layer, a tradition from the Ottoman world.
Typica
One of the oldest cultivated arabica varieties, the ancestor of many others, valued for clean, sweet cups.

U

Under-extraction
Dissolving too little from the coffee, often from grinding too coarse or brewing too briefly. It tastes sour, salty and thin.
Uniformity
In cupping, how consistent several cups of the same coffee taste, revealing defects in individual beans.

V

Vacuum brewer
Also called a siphon, a two-chamber brewer that uses vapor pressure to push water up to the coffee and a vacuum to draw it back down.
Variety
A botanical subtype of coffee plant, such as Bourbon, Typica or Gesha. "Varietal" is often used informally to mean the same thing.

W

Washed process
Also called wet process, a method in which fruit is removed and beans are fermented and washed before drying. It typically gives clean, bright, transparent flavors.
Water hardness
The mineral content of water, mainly calcium and magnesium. Moderate hardness helps extraction, while very hard water causes scale.
Wet hulling
A processing method common in Indonesia in which parchment is removed while beans are still moist, producing earthy, heavy-bodied coffees.

Y

Yield
The weight of liquid coffee produced, especially for espresso, such as 36 g from an 18 g dose.

Frequently asked questions

What does extraction mean in coffee?

Extraction is the process of dissolving flavor compounds from ground coffee into water. Too little gives sour, thin coffee and too much gives bitter, dry coffee, so brewers adjust grind, time and temperature to hit a balanced middle.

What is the difference between a variety and an origin?

A variety is the botanical type of coffee plant, such as Bourbon or Typica, while the origin is where the coffee was grown. The same variety can taste quite different in different origins.

What does single origin mean?

Single origin means the coffee comes from one place rather than a blend of regions. It can describe a country, a region, a cooperative or a single farm, so check the label for detail.

What is a good starting brew ratio?

For filter coffee, many brewers start around 1:15 to 1:17, meaning 1 g of coffee to 15–17 g of water. Espresso commonly starts near 1:2.
