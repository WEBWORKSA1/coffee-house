Cleaning and Descaling Coffee Equipment: Complete Guide

Old coffee oils and limescale quietly ruin flavor and shorten equipment life. These routines keep every brewer, grinder and machine tasting clean and working well.

9 min readBy the Coffee.House editorial teamUpdated October 2026How we write

Coffee contains oils that cling to every surface they touch. Over days they go rancid, giving brews a bitter, stale, metallic edge you might blame on the beans. At the same time, minerals in water build limescale inside kettles and machines, slowing heating, clogging valves and causing breakdowns. Cleaning removes coffee residue; descaling removes mineral scale. Both are essential, and both are easier as habits than as rescue jobs.

Cleaning vs descaling

TaskWhat it removesTypical agentsApplies to
CleaningCoffee oils, grounds, milk residueHot water, mild detergent, espresso machine detergent, milk system cleanerBrewers, portafilters, grinders, wands, carafes
DescalingCalcium and magnesium scaleCitric acid, lactic acid or manufacturer-approved descalerKettles, espresso machines, drip brewers

How often you descale depends heavily on water. Hard, alkaline water scales quickly; filtered or softer water slows it dramatically. See our water for coffee guide to understand your supply.

Espresso machines

After every use

  • Flush the group head for a few seconds to rinse away grounds.
  • Knock out the puck, rinse and wipe the basket and portafilter.
  • Purge the steam wand immediately before and after steaming, and wipe it with a damp cloth so milk does not bake on.

Weekly

  • Backflush with water using a blind basket if your machine has a three-way solenoid valve.
  • Soak baskets and portafilter spouts in hot water with espresso machine detergent; keep plastic or wooden handles out of the solution.
  • Wipe the group gasket area and shower screen with a group brush.
  • Empty and wash the drip tray and water reservoir.

Monthly and periodic

  • Backflush with espresso machine detergent, following the product's instructions, then rinse with several water-only cycles.
  • Remove and clean the shower screen and dispersion block if your machine allows.
  • Descale according to the manufacturer's schedule and method; some dual-boiler and heat-exchanger machines should not be descaled at home and need professional service.
  • Replace group gaskets when they harden, often every 6–12 months for frequent use.

Warning: Never backflush a machine without a three-way valve; it can force water into places it should not go. Always check your manual before backflushing or descaling.

Drip coffee makers

Drip machines are easy to neglect because most of their plumbing is hidden. Slow brewing, a sputtering sound, cooler coffee or a longer cycle time are classic signs of scale. Wash the carafe and filter basket after each use with warm water and mild detergent. Leave the lid open so the reservoir dries. Descale every one to three months by running a citric acid solution or approved descaler through a brew cycle, pausing halfway to let it work if the instructions allow, then running two or three cycles of fresh water. Thermal carafes benefit from an occasional soak with a coffee equipment cleaner to remove oil films.

Kettles

Scale in kettles is easy to see as white or chalky deposits. Fill the kettle partway with water and a dissolved citric acid solution, roughly 1–2 tablespoons per liter as a common household approach, or follow a commercial descaler's directions. Heat to just below boiling, let it sit for 15–30 minutes, empty, and rinse thoroughly before reuse. For electric gooseneck models, never submerge the base. Our pour-over kettles guide covers more about kettle care.

Grinders

Grinders collect oily grounds that turn rancid and fines that affect consistency.

  1. Weekly brush-out. Run the grinder empty, unplug it, then brush the chute and accessible burr area.
  2. Deep clean every 1–2 months. If the design allows, remove the upper burr and brush both burrs, the grinding chamber and chute with a dry brush.
  3. Use grinder cleaning pellets if desired. Food-safe pellets designed for coffee grinders can help remove oil. Grind a small amount of coffee afterward to purge residue.
  4. Avoid water and rice. Water can corrode burrs and bearings, and uncooked rice is harder than coffee and may strain some motors. Follow your manual.

For more on choosing a grinder that is easy to maintain, see our grinder buying guide.

Manual brewers

  • Pour-over drippers and Chemex: rinse after each use; wash with mild detergent regularly. Soak glass brewers in a coffee equipment cleaner if they develop a brown film.
  • French press: disassemble the plunger and mesh filter weekly so grounds and oils do not collect between the layers.
  • AeroPress: rinse the chamber and seal after use; occasionally wash and let the plunger dry fully.
  • Moka pot: rinse with hot water and dry fully after use; avoid harsh scrubbing that removes the protective layer, and replace the gasket when it hardens. Learn more in our moka pot guide.

Safe products vs risky shortcuts

Use products designed for coffee equipment, or simple food-safe acids such as citric acid. Avoid bleach and abrasive cleaners, which can damage surfaces, leave residues and void warranties. Be cautious with vinegar; many manufacturers advise against it because of odor and possible damage. Always rinse thoroughly after any cleaning or descaling agent, and run fresh water through machines before brewing again. Store cleaning products away from beans and food, and wear gloves if a product label recommends it.

Milk systems deserve particular care. Milk residue spoils quickly and can harbor bacteria, so steam wands should be purged and wiped after every use, and any automatic milk frothers or milk lines should be cleaned daily with the manufacturer's recommended cleaner.

Pro tip: Set calendar reminders for weekly and monthly tasks. Consistent light cleaning prevents the heavy buildup that leads to repairs.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I descale my coffee machine?

It depends on water hardness and use. With moderately hard water, every one to three months is common for daily use; soft or filtered water extends the interval. Always follow the manufacturer's guidance.

Can I descale with vinegar?

Many manufacturers advise against vinegar because it can leave lingering odors and may not be suitable for some internal parts. Citric acid or a descaler designed for coffee equipment is usually a better choice.

What is backflushing?

Backflushing forces water, and sometimes espresso machine detergent, back through the group head using a blind basket. It is only suitable for machines with a three-way solenoid valve.

How do I clean a coffee grinder?

Brush out burrs and the chute regularly, and periodically remove the upper burr for deeper cleaning if the design allows. Food-safe grinder cleaning pellets can help remove oily residue.
