    Why timing matters

    Contact time between water and coffee is one of the four main extraction variables, alongside grind size, temperature and agitation. In percolation methods like pour-over and Chemex, total time is mostly a result of grind size: if your drawdown finishes much faster than the target, grind finer; if it stalls, grind coarser. In immersion methods like the French press and AeroPress, time is a variable you control directly.

    A consistent pour schedule also controls agitation. Pouring in steady spirals at the same pace each day keeps the coffee bed level and the extraction even, which is why recipes specify both the target weight and the moment each pour begins.

    Reading the schedule

    Each line shows an action and the time it starts. The highlighted line is what you should be doing now; struck-through lines are finished. Pair the timer with the brew ratio calculator to scale any recipe to your cup size, and see the pour-over guide for technique details.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a bloom in pour-over coffee?

    The bloom is the first small pour — usually about twice the coffee's weight in water — that lets trapped carbon dioxide escape from fresh grounds for 30–45 seconds, so later pours extract evenly.

    Does the timer keep running if I switch tabs?

    Yes. The timer measures real elapsed time, so it stays accurate even if your phone screen dims or you switch apps briefly.

    Can I use the timer for my own recipe?

    Use the method closest to yours and treat each step as a cue. The timings are proven starting points; adjust to your grinder and beans.
