Flat White Recipe: Strong, Silky and Small

The flat white is a small, coffee-forward milk drink with a thin, glossy layer of microfoam. It sits between a cortado and a latte and rewards precise milk texturing.

4 min readBy the Coffee.House editorial teamUpdated October 2026How we write
Yield1 drink (about 160 ml)
Time5 min
DifficultyMedium
1×

Ingredients

  • 18–20 g finely ground espresso coffee (double ristretto or double espresso)
  • 120 ml cold whole milk

Method

  1. Preheat a 150–180 ml cup with hot water and empty it.
  2. Pull a double ristretto of about 25–30 g, or a double espresso of about 36 g, into the cup.
  3. Pour the cold milk into a small steaming jug.
  4. Stretch the milk very briefly, 2–3 seconds, so it grows by only about 5–10 percent.
  5. Submerge the tip and spin the milk until it reaches 55–62°C.
  6. Tap and swirl until the milk is glossy and uniform with no visible bubbles.
  7. Pour low and close to the surface so the milk integrates with the crema and leaves a thin, flat layer of foam, ideally with a small latte art pattern.

A flat white is the barista's milk drink: small, strong and silky, with a thin skin of microfoam that lets the espresso's flavor carry through. It is the drink that most clearly shows whether a café's espresso and milk technique are both on point.

What it is and where it comes from

The flat white is generally credited to Australia and New Zealand, where cafés were serving it by the 1980s; both countries claim it, and the exact origin remains a friendly dispute. It traveled with the international specialty café scene and is now on menus worldwide. Recipes vary a little from café to café, but the common thread is a smaller drink than a latte, a strong double shot, and minimal foam.

Flavor and texture

Expect a pronounced espresso flavor softened by sweet, warm milk. Many cafés use a ristretto, a shorter and more concentrated shot, which gives a syrupy, sweet intensity that stands up to the milk. The milk itself should be dense and velvety, almost like melted ice cream, with a glossy surface that ripples rather than holds peaks.

Technique notes

The art is in adding very little air. Stretch for just a couple of seconds, then focus on rolling the milk to break any bubbles into a fine microfoam. Slightly lower temperatures, around 55–62°C, keep the milk sweet and the texture fluid. When pouring, start a few centimeters above the cup to blend the milk under the crema, then bring the jug spout close to the surface to finish. Our latte art basics article explains the movements in more detail.

Variations

  • Single-origin flat white: try a fruity, medium-roast espresso; its brightness tastes like berries and cream with milk.
  • Oat flat white: use a barista-style oat milk and steam to around 55°C so it stays smooth.
  • AeroPress version: make a strong concentrate using our AeroPress guide and top it with frothed milk for a home approximation.
  • Larger serving: some cafés use a triple shot with a slightly larger volume of milk.

Common mistakes

Too much air is the main problem; it turns a flat white into a small cappuccino. Using a large cup dilutes the coffee and defeats the purpose of the drink. Very long, light espresso shots can also taste thin under milk, so lean toward shorter, richer extractions.

Serving tips

Serve in a warmed 150–180 ml ceramic cup or small tumbler glass, right after pouring. If you want something even more concentrated, a cortado halves the milk again.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a flat white and a latte?

A flat white is smaller, usually 150–180 ml, with a higher ratio of coffee to milk and a very thin foam layer. A latte is larger and milder.

Is a flat white stronger than a cappuccino?

It tends to taste stronger because the thin, dense microfoam lets the espresso flavor come through more directly, even though the two drinks can be similar in size.

Why is it called a flat white?

The name is generally understood to describe the flat, level surface of the drink compared with the domed foam of a traditional cappuccino.
