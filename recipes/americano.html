Americano Recipe: Espresso Lengthened With Hot Water

An Americano is espresso diluted with hot water to the strength of a filter coffee. It keeps espresso's depth and body in a longer, gentler cup.

3 min readBy the Coffee.House editorial teamUpdated October 2026How we write
Yield1 drink (about 200 ml)
Time3 min
DifficultyEasy
1×

Ingredients

  • 18 g finely ground espresso coffee (double shot, about 36 g liquid)
  • 120–180 ml hot water at 85–90°C

Method

  1. Preheat a 250 ml cup with hot water and empty it.
  2. Add 120–180 ml of hot water at 85–90°C to the cup.
  3. Pull a double espresso of about 36 g in 25–32 seconds directly onto the water.
  4. Taste and add more hot water if you prefer it lighter.
  5. Serve black, or add a splash of milk if you like.

The Americano is one of the simplest espresso drinks: a double shot topped up with hot water. It gives you a full mug of black coffee with espresso's depth, body and caramelized sweetness, without the intensity of drinking a shot straight.

What it is and where it comes from

A popular story holds that the Americano got its name in Italy during the mid-twentieth century, when visitors used to large cups of drip coffee diluted their espresso with water. The tale is widely repeated but hard to verify. Whatever the origin, the drink is now a staple of café menus around the world. A close cousin, often called a long black, reverses the order, adding espresso on top of water, and is usually a little shorter and stronger.

Flavor and texture

A good Americano tastes round and full, with the chocolate, caramel or fruit tones of the espresso stretched into a longer drink. It has more body than most filter coffee and can carry a thin layer of crema. Because espresso emphasizes heavier, roastier compounds, an Americano made from the same beans will taste different from a pour-over: deeper and less delicate.

Technique notes

Water temperature matters more than people expect. Water straight off a rolling boil can make the drink taste harsh and scorched, so aim for 85–90°C. Adding the water first, then pulling the shot on top, preserves more crema and prevents the espresso from hitting a hot, empty cup. Start with a ratio of about 1:4, espresso to water, and adjust from there. If your shots are sour or bitter, the Americano will be too; dial in using our espresso at home guide.

Variations

  • Long black: less water, about 60–100 ml, with espresso poured on top for a stronger, crema-topped cup.
  • Iced Americano: fill a glass with ice and cold water, then pour the espresso over it.
  • Americano with milk: add 20–30 ml of warm or cold milk for a softer finish.
  • Stovetop Americano: dilute strong moka pot coffee with hot water if you do not own an espresso machine.

Common mistakes

Using boiling water is the most common fault. Using a single shot in a large mug is the second; the drink ends up thin and watery. Finally, many people use dark roasts by default, but medium roasts often make a sweeter, more interesting Americano.

Serving tips

Serve in a preheated 250–300 ml mug. Because it is mostly water, an Americano is an excellent showcase for your water quality, so filtered water with moderate mineral content makes a noticeable difference; see our water for coffee page for details.

Frequently asked questions

Should water or espresso go first in an Americano?

Many baristas add hot water first and pull the espresso on top, which keeps more of the crema intact. Pouring espresso first and adding water also works and tastes very similar.

What is the ratio of water to espresso in an Americano?

Common ratios range from 1:2 to 1:5 espresso to water. A double shot with 120–180 ml of hot water is a good starting point.

How is an Americano different from drip coffee?

An Americano is made by diluting espresso, so it has more body and a richer, rounder flavor with some crema. Drip coffee is brewed at a lower concentration from the start and usually tastes cleaner and lighter.
