Affogato Recipe: Espresso Poured Over Gelato

An affogato is a scoop of cold gelato drowned in hot espresso. Two ingredients, under two minutes, and one of the most satisfying desserts in the coffee world.

3 min readBy the Coffee.House editorial teamUpdated October 2026How we write
Yield1 dessert
Time3 min
DifficultyEasy
1×

Ingredients

  • 1–2 scoops (about 70–100 g) vanilla or fior di latte gelato
  • 18 g finely ground espresso coffee (double shot, about 36–40 g liquid)
  • Shaved dark chocolate or chopped toasted nuts (optional)

Method

  1. Chill a small glass or bowl in the freezer for 10 minutes.
  2. Place a firm scoop of gelato in the chilled glass and return it to the freezer while you make the coffee.
  3. Pull a double espresso into a small jug or cup.
  4. Serve the gelato and the espresso together, and pour the espresso over the gelato at the table.
  5. Garnish with chocolate or nuts if desired and eat immediately with a spoon.

The affogato is coffee as dessert: a scoop of creamy gelato with a shot of hot espresso poured over it. As the coffee hits the ice cream it melts into a bittersweet, café-au-lait-colored pool that you eat with a spoon and finish by drinking.

What it is and where it comes from

The affogato is an Italian classic; the name means "drowned." It is served in cafés and gelato shops and is a common end to a meal, sitting somewhere between a dessert and an after-dinner coffee. Its precise origins are unclear, but it is a natural combination in a country with deep traditions of both espresso and gelato.

Flavor and texture

The appeal is contrast: hot and cold, bitter and sweet, liquid and creamy. The espresso softens the edges of the gelato, while the cold cream tames the coffee's bitterness and adds sweetness. Within a minute you get three textures in one glass: firm ice cream, a soft melting layer and a sweet coffee cream at the bottom.

Technique notes

Everything depends on temperature and timing. Chill the glass so the gelato does not melt before the espresso arrives, and scoop it firm. Pull the espresso fresh and pour it immediately; espresso that has sat for minutes loses its aroma and crema. A rich, chocolatey or nutty espresso pairs best with vanilla, while a fruitier shot can be lovely with plain milk-flavored gelato. Our espresso at home guide covers how to pull a great shot. Without a machine, use a strong concentrate from a moka pot.

Variations

  • Chocolate affogato: use chocolate or hazelnut gelato and top with shaved dark chocolate.
  • Salted caramel: drizzle caramel sauce and a pinch of flaky salt over the gelato before pouring.
  • Cold brew affogato: pour chilled cold brew concentrate over the ice cream for a slower-melting summer version.
  • Dairy-free: use a coconut or oat-based frozen dessert.
  • Crunchy topping: add crushed amaretti biscuits, toasted almonds or pistachios.

Common mistakes

Using a warm glass or soft ice cream leaves you with sweet coffee soup within seconds. Too much espresso drowns the gelato completely; one double shot per one or two scoops is plenty. Light, airy ice creams also melt much faster than dense gelato.

Serving tips

Serve the espresso on the side in a small pitcher so each guest can pour at the table; it is part of the ritual. Offer a small spoon and a glass of water. For a coffee-forward dinner party, an affogato makes a simple finish after a meal, and it is a natural way to share a coffee you love. For another cold espresso treat, try our iced latte.

Frequently asked questions

What does affogato mean?

Affogato is Italian for drowned, describing the ice cream drowned in espresso.

Is gelato or ice cream better for affogato?

Either works. Gelato is denser and less airy, so it melts more slowly and tastes more intense. Premium ice cream with low air content gives a similar result.

Can I make an affogato without an espresso machine?

Yes. Use about 50 ml of very strong coffee from a moka pot or an AeroPress concentrate. It should be hot and concentrated so it does not taste watery against the ice cream.
